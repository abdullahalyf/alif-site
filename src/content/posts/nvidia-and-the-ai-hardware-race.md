*This is my opinion as a student and developer. It is not investment advice.*

When people talk about the AI boom, one company keeps coming up: NVIDIA. Its graphics chips, called GPUs, turned out to be very good at the kind of maths that AI models need: many simple calculations running side by side. That is why the companies training large models compete to buy them.

## More than a chip

I think the chip is only half of the story. NVIDIA also built CUDA, the software that developers use to program its GPUs, and years of tools and libraries grew around it. Switching to another chip means rewriting and re-testing code, and that habit is hard to break. Hardware can be copied faster than an ecosystem.

## Who else is in the race

Other companies are trying to close the gap. AMD makes competing GPUs. Large cloud companies design their own AI chips to lower their costs. Many start-ups are betting on new designs. I don't know which of them will succeed, and I don't trust anyone who says they do.

## Risks worth watching

- **Concentration.** Most advanced chips are made by a small number of factories, so a problem in one place affects everyone.
- **Politics.** Export rules between countries can change who is able to buy what.
- **Demand.** If AI spending slows down, chip makers feel it first. Booms in technology do not always last at the same speed.

## What it means for a developer in Bangladesh

For me the practical point is this: I rarely touch the hardware, but I pay for it through cloud prices and API costs. When chips get cheaper or models get more efficient, tools I could not afford become possible for small businesses here. That is the part I follow most closely.

I may be wrong about some of this, and the picture changes quickly. If you disagree, I would like to hear it.
