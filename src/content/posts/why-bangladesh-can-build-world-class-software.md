*This is my personal view as a student in Dhaka, not a report.*

I often hear that real software companies are built somewhere else. I don't agree, and I want to say why, while being honest about what is hard here.

## What we have

- **Young people who are eager to learn.** Many of us teach ourselves from free material online, and many begin client work while still at university.
- **A freelancing culture.** Developers here already work with clients abroad and at home, so they learn to deliver and to communicate.
- **Real problems close by.** Shops, factories and farms in Bangladesh still run on paper or on tools that do not fit them. If you build something that works in this setting, you understand a hard problem well.

## What gets in the way

- Internet and power are not always reliable, which is one reason I build software that keeps working offline.
- Online payments and international banking are harder than they should be.
- Good mentorship and engineering experience are still concentrated in a few places.
- Many people learn tools and skip fundamentals such as databases, networks and system design.

## What "world-class" means to me

I don't mean a famous name. I mean software that is correct, simple to use and keeps working when conditions are poor. That is a standard anyone can meet with care, wherever they live.

## What I'm doing about it

I focus on fundamentals, build for real businesses and write down what I learn, including on this site. I think the best way to show that something can be done is to do it carefully, one project at a time.

I'm interested in other views, especially from people who have seen this from other countries.
